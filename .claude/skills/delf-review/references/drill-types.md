# Drill types

Exercises are written fresh each time. Pick the type that makes the learner produce the form, not recognise it.

| Type | How it looks | Good for | Grading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gap fill | A sentence with one blank, base form in brackets when needed | conjugation, prepositions, pronouns, connectors | exact form; accept a second correct option |
| Transformation | "Mettez au passé", "Reliez avec un pronom relatif", "Mettez à la forme négative" | tenses, relatives, negation, pronouns | the target structure must be right; ignore unrelated slips but mention them |
| Error correction | A sentence with one planted error of the learner's type; some sentences have none | agreement, élision, spelling, prepositions | finding and fixing the error; wrongly "fixing" a correct sentence counts as wrong |
| Translation NL/EN → FR | One short sentence that triggers the point in the learner's first language | prepositions, false friends, idioms, negation | the target point only; several wordings can be right |
| Choose and justify | Two forms, pick one and say why in five words | imparfait vs passé composé, indicatif vs subjonctif | choice and reason both needed |
| Mini production | "Write two sentences about X using Y" | connectors, relatives, new vocabulary | point used correctly and naturally |
| Recall | Meaning or definition given, learner supplies the French word with its article | vocabulary | word, gender and spelling |

## Building a good item

- One difficulty per item. Keep the rest of the sentence easy and at B1 vocabulary.
- Sentences sound like real life on a B1 theme, not like grammar-book filler.
- Mix in one item where the usual trap does not apply, so the learner cannot answer mechanically.
- Order: recognition first (error correction), then production (transformation, translation, mini production).
- 2 items per point in a warm-up, 3 to 4 in a full review, 6 to 8 after a lesson.
