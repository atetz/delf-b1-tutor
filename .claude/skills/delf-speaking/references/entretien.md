# Part 1: entretien dirigé (`tache/po-entretien`)

2 to 3 minutes, no preparation. The examiner opens, the candidate introduces themself, then answers follow-up
questions. It tests talking about oneself in the present, the past and the future.

## How to run it

1. Open: « Bonjour. Pouvez-vous vous présenter ? »
2. Let the learner speak until they stop. Then ask 4 to 6 follow-up questions, one at a time, built on what they
   said and on `learner/profile.md`.
3. Cover the three time frames: at least one question about now, one about the past, one about plans.
4. Close: « Très bien, je vous remercie. Nous passons à la deuxième partie. »

## Question bank (reword every time, adapt to the profile)

| Frame | Angles |
|---|---|
| Present | job or studies and what a normal day looks like; where they live and what they like about it; family, friends; free time; what they read, watch or listen to |
| Past | how they came to their job or city; a trip or a holiday that stood out; how they started learning French; what they did last weekend; a choice they are glad they made |
| Future | plans for the next holidays; where they see themselves in five years; what they will do with French; a project they would like to start |
| Opinion-light | what they would change in their town; what they prefer, working alone or in a team, and why |

Follow up like a person, not a list: « Pourquoi ? », « Et c'était comment ? », « Vous y êtes resté combien de temps ? »

## What a good answer does

- Self-introduction of about a minute without waiting for questions: name, where from and where now, work,
  family or household, interests, why French.
- Answers of two or three sentences: the fact, then a detail or a reason. Not one word, not a speech.
- Tenses follow the question: passé composé and imparfait for the past question, futur or « j'aimerais » for plans.
- When a word is missing: reword (« c'est une personne qui… », « c'est comme… ») and keep going.

## Common losses

- Stopping after name, age and job: the examiner then has to pull every sentence out.
- Answering a past question in the present.
- A memorised text recited fast, then short broken answers to the follow-ups. The contrast costs points.
