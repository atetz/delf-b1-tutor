# Part 2: exercice en interaction (`tache/po-interaction`)

3 to 4 minutes, no preparation. The candidate draws a short situation and plays it with the examiner. There is
always a problem to solve or a disagreement to settle. It tests the speech acts: explain, ask, refuse, insist,
propose, accept, thank.

## Scenario card (write a fresh one each time)

```
Situation : <two sentences: where you are, what went wrong or what you want>
Votre rôle : <who the learner is and what they must obtain>
L'examinateur joue : <the other person>
```

Hidden from the learner, for the examiner:
- **Objection:** why the other person first says no or offers too little.
- **Second obstacle:** something that comes up after the first solution (a date that does not work, a cost).
- **Acceptable outcome:** what you will agree to if the learner argues and proposes something reasonable.

## Scenario families

| Family | Example seeds | Register |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint to a service | hotel room not as booked, parcel arrived damaged, wrong bill, course cancelled without notice | vous |
| Negotiation at work | asking for a day of télétravail, swapping a shift, refusing extra work before a holiday | vous or tu, depending on the colleague |
| Problem between neighbours or flatmates | noise at night, shared cleaning, a parking space, a party planned | vous with a neighbour, tu with a flatmate |
| Organising with a friend | choosing a holiday destination with different budgets, planning a surprise party, cancelling at the last minute | tu |
| Convincing someone | a friend who wants to drop a course, a parent hesitating to buy a smartphone, a colleague to join a carpool | tu or vous |
| Asking a favour that costs | borrowing a car, asking a neighbour to keep a pet, asking a landlord for repairs | depends |

## Playing the other person

- Start cooperative but not helpful: listen, then object.
- Refuse the first request or offer something clearly smaller. Give in only to an argument or a counter-proposal.
- Bring the second obstacle once the first is solved.
- Keep turns to one or two sentences so the learner does most of the talking.
- End naturally when an agreement is reached or after about eight exchanges.

## What a good performance does

1. Greets and explains the situation clearly before asking for anything.
2. States what they want, politely: « Je voudrais… », « Est-ce qu'il serait possible de… ».
3. Does **not** accept the first offer if it does not solve the problem: « Je comprends, mais… », « Ça ne me
   convient pas vraiment, parce que… ».
4. Argues with a reason, then proposes an alternative: « Et si… ? », « On pourrait peut-être… ».
5. Checks the agreement at the end and thanks: « Donc, on est d'accord : … ».

## Useful moves

| Act | Formal (vous) | Informal (tu) |
|---|---|---|
| Explain a problem | Je vous appelle parce que… / Il y a un souci avec… | Écoute, j'ai un problème avec… |
| Ask | Pourriez-vous… ? / Serait-il possible de… ? | Tu pourrais… ? / Ça te dérangerait de… ? |
| Refuse or insist | Je regrette, mais ce n'est pas suffisant. / J'insiste, parce que… | Non, franchement, ça ne va pas. / Attends, ce n'est pas juste. |
| Propose | Je vous propose de… / Et si vous… ? | Et si on… ? / On n'a qu'à… |
| Concede | D'accord, à condition que… | Bon, d'accord, mais alors… |
| Close | Très bien, je vous remercie de votre compréhension. | Super, merci, ça marche ! |

## Common losses

- Accepting the first compromise: the exchange ends after three turns and nothing was negotiated.
- Wrong or mixed tu/vous for the relationship on the card.
- Explaining the problem at length but never saying what they want.
