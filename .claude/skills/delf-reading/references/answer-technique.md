# Reading: answer technique

Our own notes, refined from what the learner's sessions show. Add to them when a new pattern of lost marks appears.

## Order of work

1. **Questions first, for 60 seconds.** They are in the order of the text and tell you what to look for.
2. **One fast read** of the whole text for the frame: what is it about, who speaks, what is the writer's position.
3. **Question by question:** find the passage, reread only that passage, answer, move on.
4. **Last two minutes:** check nothing is blank and every vrai/faux has both parts.

## Matching exercise (`comprehension/ce-reperage`)

- Turn each requirement into something checkable: a number, a day, a yes or no.
- Take one requirement at a time across all options, not one option at a time. It is faster and avoids rereading.
- Do the arithmetic the document hides: per-person prices, totals, durations, « à partir de », « jusqu'à ».
- Small words carry the refusal: sauf, uniquement, hors, à condition de, sur réservation, complet.
- One « ne convient pas » eliminates an option, but still fill the whole grid: each cell is marked.

## Vrai / faux with justification (`comprehension/ce-justification`)

- **Write V or F first, then the proof.** A justification with no V/F earns nothing, and the reverse loses half.
- The proof is a quotation: the shortest piece of the text that decides, copied exactly, in quotation marks.
  Not your own explanation, not the whole paragraph.
- Check the quotation proves *this* statement. A sentence can be true and from the right paragraph and still
  prove something else.
- For « faux », quote the words that contradict the statement.
- Watch for statements that change one element: the person, the time, the quantity, or turn « certains » into « tous ».

## Multiple choice

- Cover the options, answer in your head from the passage, then pick the option that matches.
- Be suspicious of an option that repeats the text word for word: the right answer is usually reworded.
- Eliminate: one option is often clearly off, one is true but answers another question.

## Short answers

- Answer what is asked: « pourquoi » wants a reason, « comment » a means, « dans quel but » an aim.
- Give one complete element, lifted or lightly adapted from the text. One word is usually too short to be
  unambiguous; three lines is too long to be precise.

## Unknown words

- Skip a word that no question depends on.
- Otherwise use the sentence: what kind of word is it, is the context positive or negative, is there an example
  or a rewording right after (« c'est-à-dire », a colon, brackets)?
- Look for a familiar word inside (prefix re-, in-, dé-; a family you know).

## Opinions and facts

- Mark who says what while reading: the journalist, person A, person B.
- Signals of opinion: selon, d'après, estime, regrette, se réjouit, craint. Signals of contrast: pourtant,
  cependant, en revanche, malgré. The sentence after a contrast word often holds an answer.
