# Listening: documents, question types and technique

## Documents for exam mode (write fresh scripts)

| Doc | Kind | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everyday conversation between two people (friends, colleagues, customer and employee) | 150–180 words | a plan, a problem and its solution, with times, places, prices |
| 2 | Radio item: short interview or news report | 200–250 words | a local initiative, a trend, one or two people quoted |
| 3 | Longer report or interview on a general-interest topic | 280–330 words | facts, an opinion for and one against, a figure or two |

Writing scripts for the ear: short sentences, spoken connectors (alors, du coup, en fait), natural repetition.
Include what the questions will need: a number said once, a detail that is corrected (« non, finalement c'est
jeudi »), an opinion that differs between speakers, a distractor that is mentioned but not chosen.

## Question types

| Type | Tests | Id when missed |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice, 3 options, general | topic, purpose, who is speaking to whom, overall attitude | `comprehension/co-global` |
| Multiple choice, 3 options, detail | a time, price, place, reason, what someone decided | `comprehension/co-details` |
| Vrai / faux | a precise statement, often with a negation or a changed detail | `comprehension/co-details` |
| Who said what | matching an opinion or action to a speaker | `comprehension/co-details` |
| Short answer (a number, a word) | figures, dates, names of things | `comprehension/co-details` |
| Opinion or feeling | whether a speaker is for, against, worried, enthusiastic, and the clue | `comprehension/co-global` |

Rules for writing questions:
- Follow the order of the recording.
- Reword: the right option never repeats the recording's exact words; a wrong option often does.
- Every wrong option is plausible and tied to something actually said.
- One or two global questions per document, the rest detail.
- Radio mode: 8 questions on the transcript, same mix, with at least two on numbers or dates and two on who did what.

## Technique for the learner

**Before listening (the reading minute)**
- Read every question and underline the question word: qui, quand, combien, pourquoi.
- Guess the situation from the questions; note what kind of answer is expected (a number, a place, a reason).

**First listening: the frame**
- Who is speaking, to whom, about what, and how it ends.
- Answer the global questions; put a dot next to detail answers you think you heard.

**Second listening: the details** (`comprehension/co-details`)
- Numbers: write them as digits the moment you hear them. Watch 70s and 90s (soixante-dix, quatre-vingt-dix),
  and pairs that sound alike (deux/douze, six/dix, cinq/cent, treize/seize).
- Who did what: note initials next to each fact. In a dialogue, opinions usually differ, and a question will ask whose is whose.
- Corrections and negations: « non, en fait », « finalement », « ne… plus », « sauf » flip the answer. The first thing said is often the distractor.
- Never leave a blank: a guess costs nothing.

**When lost**
- Let the sentence go and pick up at the next one. Hunting for a missed word loses the following three.
- Lean on what you understood globally to eliminate options (`comprehension/co-global`).

**Between sessions**
- Shadowing: replay one minute with the transcript, speaking along with the voice.
- Numbers dictation: two minutes of phone numbers, prices and dates, written as digits.
